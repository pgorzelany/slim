# Core 1A direct reduction freeze evidence

Date: 2026-07-22
Platform: macOS arm64
Scope: direct reduction and bounded semantic facts; no source syntax, separate
IR language, default code-generation pass, or unrelated optimization

## Implemented boundary

- `selfhost/analysis.slim` derives stable type, ownership, scope, use,
  last-use, and dependency facts directly from canonical checked SLIM. It
  stores at most 64 binding facts per function and marks truncation.
- `selfhost/reduce.slim` traverses the same token tree and admits only the
  total rules frozen in D0028 and `docs/REDUCTION.md`.
- Reduction performs at most eight closure passes. A module that has not
  stabilized falls back to its canonical unchanged form, making one invocation
  idempotent. Dead-binding searches inspect at most 64 tokens and otherwise
  retain the binding.
- `slimc reduce` emits ordinary canonical SLIM. `slimc analyze` emits a
  versioned non-executable report. Both reuse normal checking and diagnostics;
  neither is part of ordinary `check` or `emit-c`.
- Governance rejects a second `.sil`/`.slir` input representation and rejects
  accidental integration of these optional passes into default C generation.

## Behavior and robustness gate

The exact reduction fixture and preservation fixture prove repeated-byte
idempotence and equal native exit status, stdout, and stderr before and after
reduction. The checks include visible allocation and I/O, a subsequent checked
overflow trap, and allocation failures at the first three allocation ordinals.
A deeply nested case exercises the eight-pass conservative fallback. Invalid
source produces the same stable diagnostic contract as normal compilation.

The full corpus contains 61 standalone and project fixtures with no fallback.
All 2,000 deterministic malformed-input mutations reject without a compiler
trap; 100 are additionally sent through both Core 1A tools.

## Process-level scaling

The geometric quick benchmark builds the production compiler once, then
measures separate compiler processes. Times are medians in microseconds and
include launch and source I/O. Both new tools remain under the required 1.25
scaling exponent.

| declarations | source bytes | reduce us | analyze us | reduced bytes | analysis bytes |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 250 | 17,612 | 5,370 | 4,529 | 17,595 | 54,695 |
| 500 | 35,362 | 8,383 | 7,001 | 35,345 | 109,449 |
| 1,000 | 70,862 | 15,128 | 11,758 | 70,845 | 220,949 |
| 2,000 | 143,862 | 27,990 | 21,222 | 143,845 | 444,949 |

The same before/after process-level check samples show no material large-input
regression because neither tool is called by the ordinary path:

| declarations | check before us | check after us | emit before us | emit after us |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 250 | 3,870 | 4,118 | 8,823 | 9,391 |
| 500 | 5,987 | 5,787 | 22,954 | 22,692 |
| 1,000 | 9,348 | 9,269 | 70,963 | 71,343 |
| 2,000 | 16,317 | 16,522 | 251,223 | 252,740 |

Reproduce the scaling gate with:

    cargo run --release --bin slim-bench -- reduction --quick

sixteen-module SLIM project. The frozen seed is 1,141,206 bytes with SHA-256
`aeef245413e5766d86ca84efdcea63f3b29694728c4b9a4cf774ce387bfe5572`.
